APA Formatting Guide for Nursing Students

Title page, headings, citations, and references explained with nursing-specific examples

July 2026 • 10 min read

By the Sleek Academia tutoring team

menu_bookPart of our Assignment Help for Nursing Students →

APA formatting is one of the easiest places to lose marks in nursing school, and one of the most frustrating. You can write a strong paper and still drop a letter grade because the title page was missing an element, a citation was formatted incorrectly, or the reference list used the wrong indentation. The good news is that APA 7th edition is a finite, learnable system. Once you set up a correct template, most of the formatting work becomes automatic.

This guide covers the elements nursing instructors actually grade: the student title page, the abstract, heading structure, in-text citations, the reference list, and worked examples for the source types you will use most, including journal articles, textbooks, and clinical guidelines.

Key Takeaways

  • Student papers do not need a running head, only a page number in the top right corner.
  • Use Level 1 and Level 2 headings to organize most nursing papers; deeper levels are rare.
  • Three or more authors become "first author et al." from the first citation onward.
  • The reference list uses a hanging indent, alphabetical order, and a DOI wherever one exists.
  • Keep sources within five years unless you are citing a seminal work.

Why APA Matters in Nursing School

Nursing programs treat APA as a professional standard, not a stylistic preference. Evidence-based practice depends on being able to trace every claim back to a credible source, and APA is the system that makes that traceability possible. When your instructor deducts points for citation errors, they are grading a clinical habit: in practice, documentation and communication errors have real consequences. Treat formatting as part of the assignment, not an afterthought, and budget time for a dedicated formatting pass before submission.

The Student Title Page

APA 7 distinguishes between professional papers and student papers. Almost everything you submit in a nursing program uses the student format, which requires seven elements, centered on the upper half of the page, double-spaced, in this order:

  1. Title of the paper, in bold, title case, three or four lines down from the top margin.
  2. Your name, without titles or degrees.
  3. Department and institution, for example: Department of Nursing, State University.
  4. Course number and name, for example: NURS 301: Pathophysiology.
  5. Instructor's name, in the form they prefer.
  6. Due date, spelled out: October 14, 2026.
  7. Page number, in the top right header, starting at 1 on the title page.

The most common title page errors are adding a running head (professional format only, not required for students), forgetting to bold the title, and abbreviating the course name. Set the whole document in a readable font such as 12-point Times New Roman or 11-point Calibri, double-spaced throughout, with one-inch margins on all sides.

The Abstract

An abstract is a single paragraph, usually 150 to 250 words, summarizing your paper's purpose, main points, and conclusion. It appears on its own page after the title page, with the bold centered heading "Abstract" at the top. The paragraph is not indented.

Not every nursing paper needs one. Short essays, discussion posts, and care plans typically skip the abstract, while research papers, literature reviews, and evidence-based practice papers usually require one. Check your rubric. When you do write an abstract, draft it last: write the paper first, then compress each major section into one or two sentences. Do not include citations in an abstract unless absolutely necessary.

Headings: The Five APA Levels

Headings give your paper a visible structure, and nursing instructors expect them in anything longer than a few pages. APA defines five levels, but most student papers need only the first two or three:

LevelFormatTypical Use
Level 1Centered, bold, title caseMajor sections: Methods, Results, Pathophysiology, Nursing Implications
Level 2Flush left, bold, title caseSubsections within a major section
Level 3Flush left, bold italic, title caseSub-subsections in longer papers
Level 4Indented, bold, title case, ending with a period; text begins on the same lineRare in student papers
Level 5Indented, bold italic, title case, ending with a period; text begins on the same lineRare in student papers

Two practical notes. First, the introduction of your paper does not get a heading; the paper's title at the top of the first body page serves that role. Second, never use a heading with only one subsection beneath it. If a section has a Level 2 heading, it needs at least two.

In-Text Citations

Every idea, statistic, or finding you borrow needs an in-text citation, whether you quote it directly or paraphrase it. APA offers two forms, and strong papers mix both:

  • Parenthetical: the citation sits at the end of the sentence, for example: Early mobilization reduces the risk of hospital-acquired pneumonia (Nguyen et al., 2024).
  • Narrative: the author is part of the sentence, for example: Nguyen et al. (2024) found that early mobilization reduces the risk of hospital-acquired pneumonia.

Three rules cover most situations you will meet. For one or two authors, cite all names every time: (Lee & Ortiz, 2023). For three or more authors, use the first author plus "et al." from the very first citation: (Kowalski et al., 2023). For direct quotations, always add a page or paragraph number: (Lee & Ortiz, 2023, p. 214). If you are citing a fact you found quoted inside another source, name the original work and add "as cited in" the source you actually read, though tracking down the original is always better practice.

Paraphrasing is where most unintentional plagiarism happens. Changing a few words is not paraphrasing. Read the source, close it, explain the idea in your own sentence structure, then cite it. If three or more consecutive words match the original, you need quotation marks.

The Reference List

The reference list starts on a new page after the body, with the bold centered heading "References". The rules that instructors check first:

  • Alphabetize by the first author's surname.
  • Apply a hanging indent: the first line of each entry is flush left, and subsequent lines are indented 0.5 inches.
  • Double-space the entire list, with no extra spacing between entries.
  • Italicize journal names and book titles; capitalize journal names in title case but article titles in sentence case.
  • Include the DOI as a full URL (https://doi.org/...) whenever one exists.
  • Every reference entry must be cited in the text, and every in-text citation must appear in the list.

Reference Examples for Common Nursing Sources

These templates cover the sources nursing students cite most often. Replace the placeholder details with your source's information:

  • Journal article: Nguyen, T. H., Ortiz, M. L., & Baker, R. J. (2024). Early mobilization and pneumonia risk in medical-surgical patients. Journal of Nursing Care Quality, 39(2), 112-120. https://doi.org/xxxx
  • Textbook: Huether, S. E., & McCance, K. L. (2023). Understanding pathophysiology (8th ed.). Elsevier.
  • Clinical practice guideline: American Diabetes Association. (2025). Standards of care in diabetes-2025. https://professional.diabetes.org/standards-of-care
  • Government website: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. (2025, March 12). Hand hygiene in healthcare settings. https://www.cdc.gov/hand-hygiene
  • Drug reference (online database): Lexicomp. (2026). Metoprolol: Drug information. UpToDate. Retrieved July 22, 2026, from https://...

Note the small details that cost marks: the ampersand before the final author, the year in parentheses, sentence case for article titles, italics on the journal name and volume number but not the issue number, and a retrieval date only for content designed to change over time.

Common APA Mistakes in Nursing Papers

  • Citing at the paragraph level. One citation at the end of a paragraph does not cover three borrowed ideas. Cite each claim where it appears.
  • Overquoting. More than one or two short quotations per page usually signals weak paraphrasing. Quote only when the exact wording matters.
  • Reference and citation mismatches. Every (Smith, 2024) in the text needs a Smith entry in the list, and vice versa.
  • Using consumer health sites as evidence. WebMD and Healthline are not peer-reviewed. Use journals, textbooks, and professional guidelines instead.
  • Inconsistent heading levels. Pick a hierarchy and apply it uniformly across the paper.

A Final Formatting Checklist

Before you submit, run this five-minute pass: title page elements in order with the title bolded; page numbers top right on every page; double spacing and one-inch margins throughout; headings at consistent levels; every borrowed idea cited at the sentence level; references alphabetized with hanging indents and DOIs; and a final scan that every citation has a matching reference entry. If you are writing a paper that also needs a strong clinical argument, such as an evidence-based practice paper or a pathophysiology paper, format the citations as you research rather than retrofitting them at the end.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do nursing papers use APA 7th edition?

Yes. Nearly all nursing programs in the United States require APA 7th edition for essays, care plans, discussion posts, and research papers. Always confirm with your course syllabus, but APA 7 is the default unless your instructor specifies otherwise.

Does a student nursing paper need a running head?

No. Under APA 7, the running head is only required for professional papers submitted for publication. Student papers need only the page number in the top right corner of every page, including the title page.

How do I cite a source with three or more authors in APA 7?

Use the first author's surname followed by et al. from the very first citation, both in narrative and parenthetical citations. For example: (Kowalski et al., 2023). In the reference list, include up to 20 authors before using an ellipsis.

How recent do sources need to be for nursing papers?

Most nursing instructors expect sources published within the last five years, because clinical guidelines and best practices change quickly. Seminal works and classic theory papers are acceptable exceptions when they are cited for their original contribution.
