How to Write a Finance Research Paper

A structured approach from topic selection to policy implications

July 2026 • 12 min read

By the Sleek Academia tutoring team

menu_bookPart of our Finance Research Help Guide →

Key Takeaways

  • check_circle Choose a specific, testable research question -- broad topics produce weak papers.
  • check_circle Ground your hypothesis in existing financial theory and empirical findings.
  • check_circle Use credible data sources and appropriate quantitative methods.
  • check_circle Connect your findings to practical implications for investors, firms, or policymakers.

A finance research paper is different from a financial analysis report. While a report examines a specific company's financial health, a research paper investigates a broader question using empirical methods. You might ask whether ESG scores affect stock returns, how interest rate changes impact corporate investment, or whether gender diversity on boards improves financial performance. The goal is to contribute evidence to an ongoing academic conversation.

Selecting a Topic and Formulating a Research Question

A good finance research topic is specific, debatable, and researchable. Avoid broad questions like "Is the stock market efficient?" which have been debated for decades with no resolution. Instead, narrow the scope: "Does the introduction of ESG reporting requirements affect the cost of equity capital for European listed firms?"

Your research question should have the following qualities: it can be answered with available data, it connects to existing theory or empirical literature, and the answer is not obvious. If you already know the answer, the question is not worth researching.

Developing a Hypothesis and Theoretical Framework

Your hypothesis is a testable statement derived from theory. For example, if you are studying the relationship between board diversity and firm performance, you might hypothesise: "Firms with greater gender diversity on their boards have higher return on assets." This hypothesis can be tested with regression analysis.

Ground your hypothesis in existing financial theory. Agency theory, signalling theory, trade-off theory, and market efficiency are common foundations for finance hypotheses. Explain the theoretical basis for your expected result before you test it. If the data contradicts your hypothesis, that is a valid and interesting finding -- but only if your theoretical framework was sound.

The Standard Finance Research Paper Structure

Section Purpose
Introduction State the research question, its importance, and the paper's contribution.
Literature Review Summarise relevant prior research and identify the gap your paper fills.
Hypothesis Development Present your hypotheses and the theoretical reasoning behind them.
Data and Methodology Describe your data sources, sample selection, variables, and empirical model.
Results Present descriptive statistics and regression results in tables.
Discussion Interpret the results and explain what they mean in the context of the literature.
Conclusion Summarise findings, note limitations, and suggest implications and future research.

Data Sources and Methodology

Finance research typically uses quantitative data from major databases. For undergraduate papers, Yahoo Finance, Bloomberg (if your university provides access), World Bank Open Data, and the Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED) are accessible sources. Your variables should include a dependent variable (what you are trying to explain), independent variables (your main explanatory factors), and control variables (other factors that could affect the result).

Describe your methodology clearly enough that another researcher could replicate it. State the sample period, the number of observations, how you handled missing data, and the regression model you used (ordinary least squares, fixed effects, logit, etc.). Defend your methodological choices -- why this model and not another?

Presenting Results

Finance papers rely heavily on tables. Present descriptive statistics first: means, medians, standard deviations, minimums, and maximums for all variables. Then present the correlation matrix to show relationships between variables. Finally, present your regression results with coefficients, standard errors, and significance levels. Each table should have a clear caption and be referred to in the text.

In the text, highlight the most important findings. Do not describe every coefficient -- focus on the ones that test your hypotheses. If a result is statistically significant, say so and explain its economic significance. A coefficient may be statistically significant but economically trivial if the effect size is very small.

Discussion and Policy Implications

The discussion section is where you explain what your results mean. How do they support or contradict existing literature? What are the practical implications for investors, corporate managers, or policymakers? If you found that ESG disclosure reduces the cost of capital, what does that mean for companies considering voluntary disclosure? For regulators designing disclosure requirements?

Be honest about limitations. No study is perfect. Acknowledge data limitations, potential endogeneity, omitted variable bias, or the limited generalisability of your findings. Then suggest how future research could address these limitations.

A well-written finance research paper combines rigorous methodology with clear communication. By choosing a focused question, grounding your hypothesis in theory, and presenting your results transparently, you can produce a paper that makes a genuine contribution to the field. Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good topic for a finance research paper?

Good topics are specific, researchable, and relevant to current financial issues. Examples include: the impact of ESG scores on stock returns, the effectiveness of quantitative easing during economic downturns, or the relationship between CEO compensation and firm performance in the technology sector.

What data sources do finance researchers use?

Common sources include Bloomberg, Refinitiv Eikon, Compustat, CRSP, and Yahoo Finance for market data. Macroeconomic data can be sourced from the World Bank, IMF, or Federal Reserve databases. Many universities provide access to these through the library.

Do I need to use econometric methods in my finance research paper?

Most undergraduate finance research papers require at least basic regression analysis. If your course has not covered econometrics, a paper based on ratio analysis, trend analysis, and descriptive statistics may be acceptable. Check with your lecturer.

How do I write a good hypothesis for a finance paper?

A good hypothesis is testable, specific, and grounded in theory. For example: 'Firms with higher ESG ratings have a lower cost of equity capital.' This can be tested with regression analysis and is supported by stakeholder theory.
