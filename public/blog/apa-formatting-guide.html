APA 7 Formatting Guide: Examples for Students

The rules that actually get graded — setup, headings, in-text citations, and references, with examples

July 2026 • 10 min read

By the Sleek Academia tutoring team

menu_bookBuilding a paper right now? Our research paper guide → pairs with this one.

APA style is the default citation format for nursing, psychology, education, business, and most of the social sciences. The seventh edition, released in 2020, simplified several rules that students had been tripping over for years — but instructors still deduct points for the same handful of formatting errors. This guide covers the parts of APA 7 that actually get graded in student papers: document setup, the title page, headings, in-text citations, and the reference list, each with examples you can copy and adapt.

Paper Setup: The Baseline Rules

Set these before you type a word, because retrofitting them is tedious:

  • Margins: 1 inch on all four sides.
  • Font: APA 7 allows several accessible options — 12-point Times New Roman, 11-point Calibri, 11-point Arial, or 10-point Lucida Sans Unicode. Use one font throughout.
  • Spacing: double-space everything, including the title page, headings, block quotes, and the reference list. Do not add extra blank lines between paragraphs.
  • Page numbers: top-right corner of every page, including the title page (which is page 1).
  • Paragraph alignment: left-aligned, with a 0.5-inch first-line indent. Never justify the text.

One change students still get wrong: APA 7 uses one space after a period, not two. And student papers no longer include a running head — that requirement now applies only to manuscripts submitted for publication.

The Student Title Page

The student version of the APA 7 title page is a specific seven-element layout, centered on the upper half of the first page:

  1. Paper title — bold, title case, centered, three or four lines down from the top.
  2. Author name — your full name, one blank double-spaced line below the title.
  3. Affiliation — department and college name, separated by a comma.
  4. Course number and name — for example, NURS 5334: Advanced Pathophysiology.
  5. Instructor name — as they prefer to be addressed.
  6. Due date — in the format your instructor expects, for example, October 14, 2026.
  7. Page number — 1, in the top-right header.

Abstracts are rare in student papers unless the assignment asks for one. When required, the abstract sits alone on page 2 under the bold, centered heading "Abstract," written as a single unindented paragraph of 150 to 250 words.

Headings: The Five Levels

Headings organize your argument and tell the reader where they are. APA 7 defines five levels; most student papers need only levels 1 and 2:

LevelFormat
1Centered, bold, title case. Text begins on a new paragraph.
2Flush left, bold, title case. Text begins on a new paragraph.
3Flush left, bold italic, title case. Text begins on a new paragraph.
4Indented, bold, title case, ending with a period. Text begins on the same line.
5Indented, bold italic, title case, ending with a period. Text begins on the same line.

Two details matter here. First, the paper title at the top of page one is bold but is not a level 1 heading, and you do not write the heading "Introduction" — the opening section is understood. Second, "title case" means you capitalize the major words: Levels of Heading, not Levels of heading.

In-Text Citations

Every idea you borrow — quoted or paraphrased — needs an in-text citation pointing to a reference entry. APA gives you two shapes:

  • Parenthetical: the citation sits at the end of the sentence — (Walker & Osei, 2024).
  • Narrative: the author is part of your sentence — Walker and Osei (2024) found that structured debriefing reduced medication errors.

Mix both forms through the paper. A paragraph of pure parenthetical citations reads like a database, and a paragraph of pure narrative citations reads like a book report.

How author count changes the citation

  • One author: (Kim, 2025), every time.
  • Two authors: (Kim & Alvarez, 2025), every time. Note the ampersand inside parentheses; use "and" in narrative citations.
  • Three or more authors: (Kim et al., 2025) from the first citation onward. APA 7 dropped the old list-them-all-first rule.

The special cases

  • Direct quotes need a locator: (Kim, 2025, p. 42) or (Kim, 2025, para. 6) for sources without page numbers. Quotes of 40 words or more become block quotes: indented 0.5 inch as a freestanding block, no quotation marks, citation after the final period.
  • No author: move the title into the author slot and cite a shortened title — ("New Grad Retention," 2025).
  • No date: use n.d. — (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, n.d.).
  • Multiple sources in one set of parentheses: alphabetical, separated by semicolons — (Doe, 2023; Kim, 2025).
  • Same author, same year: add letters to the year, assigned by the alphabetical order of the titles in your reference list — (Kim, 2025a) and (Kim, 2025b).
  • Group authors with long names: spell the name out with its abbreviation at the first citation — (American Nurses Association [ANA], 2025) — then use the abbreviation alone after that: (ANA, 2025).
  • Secondary sources — when you read about Smith's study inside Jones's article: (Smith, 2019, as cited in Jones, 2024). Only Jones appears in your reference list. Track down the original whenever you can; secondary citation is a compromise, not a preference.

The Reference List

The reference list starts on its own page, headed "References" — bold, centered. The rules that produce most deductions:

  • Hanging indent of 0.5 inch: the first line of each entry is flush left, and every line after it indents. In Word, this is Ctrl+T, not a chain of spaces.
  • Alphabetical order by first author surname. Nothing is numbered or bulleted.
  • Double-spaced throughout, with no extra gaps between entries.
  • Sentence case for article and book titles — capitalize only the first word, the first word after a colon, and proper nouns. Journal names keep title case and italics.
  • DOIs as URLs — https://doi.org/10.xxxx/xxxxx, with no period at the end and no "Retrieved from."
  • Up to 20 authors are listed in full; for 21 or more, list the first 19, an ellipsis, and the final author.

Two reference-list edge cases deserve mention. Personal communications — emails, interviews you conducted, private messages — are cited in the text only, as (T. Rivera, personal communication, March 3, 2026), and never appear in the reference list because the reader cannot retrieve them. And general mentions of whole websites, where you are not quoting specific content, need no citation at all — just name the site in the text and give the URL in parentheses. Everything else you quote, paraphrase, or rely on needs both halves of the system: the in-text citation and the reference entry.

Reference Examples You Can Copy

Adapt these five templates and you will cover nearly every source in a typical student paper:

Source typeReference format
Journal articleWalker, J. M., & Osei, K. (2024). Structured debriefing and medication error rates in acute care. Journal of Nursing Care Quality, 39(2), 118–126. https://doi.org/10.1097/NCQ.0000000000000712
BookPolit, D. F., & Beck, C. T. (2021). Nursing research: Generating and assessing evidence for nursing practice (11th ed.). Wolters Kluwer.
Chapter in an edited bookKim, S. (2023). Pain assessment across cultures. In R. Alvarez (Ed.), Clinical judgment in practice (pp. 88–104). Springer.
WebpageAmerican Nurses Association. (2025). Position statement: Nurse staffing. https://www.nursingworld.org/practice-policy/staffing/
Online reportNational Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine. (2021). The future of nursing 2020–2030: Charting a path to achieve health equity. National Academies Press. https://doi.org/10.17226/25982

Notice the pattern: author, date, title, source. Almost every APA reference answers those four questions in that order — who made it, when, what it is called, and where it lives.

Numbers, Abbreviations, and Other Graded Mechanics

Beyond citations, APA 7 sets conventions for the small things instructors notice:

  • Numbers: spell out zero through nine and use numerals for 10 and above. But always use numerals with units of measurement (5 mg), with ages (a 4-year-old), and for scores and scale points (rated 3 on a 7-point scale). Never open a sentence with a numeral.
  • Abbreviations: define at first use — the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) — then use the abbreviation alone. Standard abbreviations like IQ and HIV need no definition.
  • Lists: use lettered (a), (b), (c) markers for items inside a paragraph, and numbered lists only when order genuinely matters. Bulleted lists are allowed; keep the punctuation parallel.
  • Percentages: use the numeral with the % symbol almost everywhere — 18% — including at the start of sentences.
  • Bias-free language: APA 7 tightened its guidance here. Use person-first or identity-first language per community preference, singular "they" where appropriate, and specific age ranges instead of labels like "the elderly."

Paraphrasing Without Plagiarizing

APA formatting gets the spotlight, but the citation system's real job is protecting you from plagiarism — and most student plagiarism is accidental patchwriting: swapping a few words in the original sentence and calling it a paraphrase. That is still plagiarism, even with a citation attached, because the sentence structure remains the author's.

Paraphrase properly with a four-step habit: read the passage until you understand it, close the source, write the idea in your own words and structure, then reopen the source to verify accuracy and add the citation. A genuine paraphrase changes both the wording and the sentence shape while preserving the meaning. When you find yourself needing the author's exact distinctive phrase, that is what quotation marks are for — quote it sparingly, cite it with a locator, and move on. Instructors running papers through text-matching software are looking for exactly this distinction, and "I cited it" does not rescue a patchwritten paragraph.

Tables and Figures, Briefly

If your paper includes data tables or figures, APA 7 gives both the same skeleton: a bold number (Table 1), an italicized title case title on the next line, the table or figure itself, and a note underneath if anything needs explaining. Refer to every table and figure by number in the text — as shown in Table 1 — and place each one either embedded after its first mention or collected at the end, per your assignment instructions.

The Mistakes That Cost the Most Points

  • Title case in reference titles. Article and book titles take sentence case in the reference list, even though your own paper's headings use title case.
  • Listing all authors in text. Three or more authors means et al. from the first citation.
  • Missing locators on direct quotes. A quotation without a page or paragraph number is incomplete, full stop.
  • "Retrieved from" before URLs. APA 7 dropped it. Paste the plain URL or DOI link.
  • A reference list with no matching citations. Every entry must be cited in the text, and every in-text citation must appear in the list. Cross-check before you submit — this is the single most common deduction.
  • Citation generators trusted blindly. They routinely mangle capitalization, DOIs, and webpage dates. Generate, then verify against the templates above.

Your Pre-Submission APA Checklist

Sixty seconds per item, in this order:

  • One-inch margins, one approved font, double-spaced everywhere, page number top-right on every page.
  • Title page has all seven student elements, in the right order, title in bold title case.
  • Headings follow the level formats — level 1 centered bold, level 2 flush-left bold.
  • Every paraphrase and quotation has an in-text citation; every quotation has a page or paragraph locator.
  • Three-plus authors are et al. in text; up to 20 authors spelled out in the reference list.
  • Reference list starts on its own page, alphabetical, hanging indent, sentence-case titles, DOIs as full URLs.
  • Every in-text citation matches a reference entry, and every reference entry is cited in the text. Run this cross-check last, after all edits.

Master these five areas — setup, title page, headings, in-text citations, references — and APA stops being a source of lost points and becomes what it should be: invisible scaffolding under a good argument. Frequently Asked Questions

Do student papers need a running head in APA 7?

No. APA 7 removed the running head requirement for student papers. Only professional papers submitted for publication need one. Student papers just show the page number in the top-right header of every page.

When do I use et al. in APA 7?

Use et al. from the very first citation whenever a work has three or more authors. Cite only the first author's surname followed by et al. and the year, for example (Nguyen et al., 2024). In the reference list, name up to 20 authors in full.

How do I cite a source with no author in APA 7?

Move the title into the author position. In text, use a shortened version of the title in quotation marks or italics depending on the source type, plus the year, for example ("Nursing Shortage Update," 2025).

What is a DOI and when do I include it?

A DOI is a permanent digital object identifier for a published work. Include it whenever one exists, formatted as a full URL beginning with https://doi.org/. Do not add a period after the DOI, and do not write Retrieved from before it.

Does APA 7 use title case or sentence case for article titles?

Sentence case. In the reference list, capitalize only the first word of the article title, the first word after a colon, and proper nouns. Journal names, however, keep full title case and italics.
